Durrës is central in a way that is easy to miss on a map. The country is small,
the main road runs north to south past the town, and most of what people travel
to Albania for is within an hour or two. You can do any of these and still be
back on the beach in the evening.

Distances below are rough and traffic in and out of Tirana in summer can add a
good half hour, so treat them as a guide rather than a timetable.

## Kruja — about an hour

A hill town built around a castle, with a stone bazaar on the way up to it
selling the same things it has sold for a very long time. The castle holds the
Skanderbeg museum; the view down over the plain from the walls is the part
people remember.

It is the most touristed thing on this list and still worth it. Go in the
morning — the bazaar is best before the coaches arrive, and the road up is
steep and narrow.

## Tirana — about forty minutes

The capital, and much better than its reputation. The centre is walkable, the
old communist-era buildings have been painted in colours that make no sense and
work anyway, and the Bunk'Art museums inside real bunkers are the most
interesting thing to do indoors in Albania.

A cable car runs up Mount Dajti from the edge of the city if you want to look
back down at it.

## Divjaka-Karavasta — about an hour south

A lagoon behind a pine forest, and one of the last places in the Mediterranean
where Dalmatian pelicans breed. There is a boardwalk out over the water.

This is the quiet one on the list. If the beach has been busy and you want a day
that is the opposite of that, go here.

## Cape of Rodon — about an hour and a half

A headland with a ruined castle at the end of it and very little else, which is
the point. The last stretch of road is rough. Take water; there is nothing out
there.

---

If you are hiring a car, note that you will not need it for most of the stay —
the beach is under the balcony and the town is walkable. It is worth having for
two or three days rather than for the week.
