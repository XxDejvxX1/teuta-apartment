Durrës is more central than it looks on a map. The country is small, the main
road runs north to south past the town, and most of what people come to Albania
to see is within an hour or two. You can do any of these and still be back on
the beach in the evening.

The times below are rough. Traffic in and out of Tirana in summer can add half
an hour, so take them as a guide.

## Kruja, about an hour

A hill town built around a castle, with an old stone bazaar on the way up. The
castle holds the Skanderbeg museum, and the view over the plain from its walls
is the best part.

It is the busiest place on this list and still worth the trip. Go in the
morning, before the coaches arrive. The road up is steep and narrow.

## Tirana, about forty minutes

The capital. The centre is easy to walk, many of the old communist-era buildings
have been painted in bright colours, and the Bunk'Art museums are inside real
bunkers.

A cable car runs up Mount Dajti from the edge of the city if you want to look
back down at it.

## Divjaka-Karavasta, about an hour south

A lagoon behind a pine forest, and one of the last places in the Mediterranean
where Dalmatian pelicans breed. A boardwalk runs out over the water.

This is the quiet one. If the beach has been busy and you want a day of the
opposite, go here.

## Cape of Rodon, about an hour and a half

A headland with a ruined castle at the end of it and not much else. The last
stretch of road is rough. Take water, because there is nothing out there.

---

If you are hiring a car, you will not need it for most of the stay. The beach is
under the balcony and the town is walkable, so hire it for two or three days
rather than the week.
