We are on the first line, so the beach is not somewhere you travel to here —
it is what is on the other side of the door. That changes how you use it. You
are not committing to a day out; you can go down for forty minutes and come
back up for a coffee.

After a few seasons of watching it from the balcony, the day has a shape.

## Early — before nine

The sea is at its flattest and the sand has been cleaned. This is the only part
of the day when the water is properly still, and it is the best time to swim if
you want to actually swim rather than stand about in it. Almost nobody is out.

It is also the only time the light comes over the town rather than off the
water, which is the difference between photographs that work and photographs
that are all glare.

## The middle of the day

From about eleven the wind gets up and stays up, and by early afternoon there is
usually chop. Families with small children tend to move up the beach in this
window or go in.

Honestly, the middle of the day in July and August is for being indoors. This is
the stretch where having the apartment thirty seconds away earns itself — you
come up, eat, sit in the cool, and go back down at five when it is worth being
out again.

## Late afternoon into evening

This is the good part, and it is the reason to be on this bit of the coast
rather than the middle of the strip.

The wind drops. The water goes flat again. And because Durrës faces west, the
sun sets straight into the sea in front of you rather than behind the buildings
— you get the whole thing from the sand, or from the balcony if you would
rather not move.

After the sun is down the front fills up with people walking, which is the
evening xhiro and is a genuine local habit rather than a tourist thing. Dinner
here happens late. Nobody is eating at six.

## A note on this stretch

We are away from the central strip and its bars, which is deliberate and is why
it is quiet at night. If you have come for nightlife, it is a fifteen-minute
taxi ride and you will want to know that before you book rather than after.
