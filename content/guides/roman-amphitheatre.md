Most people come to Durrës for the water and leave without knowing that one of
the largest Roman amphitheatres in the Balkans sits a few streets back from the
port, under the houses.

It was built in the second century, when the town was called Dyrrachium and the
road to Constantinople started at its harbour. Later it was abandoned, filled
in, and forgotten so completely that people built houses on top of it for
generations. It was found again in the 1960s. You can still see where the
excavation stopped: the far side of the bowl runs into the back walls of houses
that people still live in, with washing hung out.

## What there is to see

The seating is stone and largely intact on the excavated side, and you can walk
down to the arena floor. It is quiet for something this size. Usually there are
a few people, a lot of grass, and cats.

I would not skip the small Byzantine chapel built into one of the lower
passages, centuries after the arena stopped being used. It has a wall mosaic
inside, which is an odd thing to find in the corridor of a Roman theatre.

## Making a morning of it

The Byzantine walls and the Venetian tower by the port are close enough to the
amphitheatre to see all three on one walk. The archaeological museum is on the
seafront on the way back, so you can start inland and finish by the water.

Go early. There is very little shade in the bowl, and by midday in July it holds
the heat like a pan.

## Getting there from the apartment

It is about fifteen minutes on foot, along the front and then inland, or five
minutes in a taxi. Ask anyone in town for the amfiteatri and they will point you
to it.
