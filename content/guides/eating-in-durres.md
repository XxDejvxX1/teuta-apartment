I am not going to send you to one restaurant, because the one I would name this
year may have changed hands by the time you read this and you would be standing
outside it holding my recommendation. What is worth knowing is how eating here
works, because a few of the conventions are not obvious and getting them wrong
is what makes people think Albania is expensive.

## Fish is sold by the kilo

This is the one that catches people out. At a fish restaurant you are usually
shown the catch and you choose it; the price on the menu is per kilogram, not
per plate. A sea bass big enough for two is a different number from the one you
read on the board.

None of this is a trick, and nobody minds being asked. Point at the fish, ask
what it weighs and what that comes to, and agree it before it goes on the grill.
Do that once and you will never think about it again.

## Breakfast

Byrek — thin pastry layered with cheese, spinach or meat — is what people
actually eat in the morning, bought from a bakery, standing up, for very little.
It is better than anything you will be served on a hotel buffet.

Coffee is taken seriously and drunk slowly. A macchiato is the default order and
it is small. If you ask for a large coffee you will get something nobody around
you is drinking.

## Dinner

Late. Kitchens are still busy at ten in the evening and arriving at seven means
eating alone in an empty room. Adjust the first night and the rest of the week
sorts itself out.

Worth ordering at least once:

- **Fërgesë** — peppers, tomato and cottage cheese baked together and eaten with
  bread. It is the one to have if you only have one.
- **Qofte** — grilled meatballs, flat rather than round here.
- **Speca të mbushur** — stuffed peppers.
- **Whatever is grilled and whole.** The simplest thing on the menu is usually
  the thing the kitchen is best at.

Raki arrives at the end whether or not you asked for it. It is homemade more
often than not and it is stronger than it tastes.

## Cooking in

There is a full kitchen in the apartment and a market within walking distance,
which is worth using at least once — the tomatoes and the peaches in summer are
the sort of thing you cannot buy at home at any price. Fish is best bought in
the morning.
